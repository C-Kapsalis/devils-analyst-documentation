What the benchmark measures
===========================

Devil's Analyst ships a benchmark so that its claims can be checked,
and so that a proposed check can be shown to matter. This page says
what it measures, what it has shown so far, and where its numbers can
mislead you. The full method is in the repository's
`benchmarks/METHOD.md
<https://github.com/C-Kapsalis/devils-analyst/blob/v0.1.0/benchmarks/METHOD.md>`_.

The question
------------

Does a small model handle marketing data better with Devil's Analyst
loaded than without it? "Better" means what a careful senior analyst
does before anyone moves budget: finds the problems in the data that
change the answer, reaches the right decision, ranks first the problems
that should stop a decision, leaves correct details alone, and calls
clean work clean.

How a case works
----------------

Each case is a request a marketer would send, such as "Which version
should we go with?", with exports in a folder. The model writes a
report, and graders read it. The same prompt and files run twice: once
with the plugin loaded and once without it.

``python3 scripts/count.py`` counts 110 cases in three groups:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 10 65

   * - Group
     - Cases
     - What a case is
   * - Focused
     - 62
     - A clean export built around one named trap, such as averaged
       rates, summed daily users, a sample ratio mismatch or a
       promotion's pull-forward.
   * - Breadth
     - 36
     - A realistic draft report, memo or proposal with the exports
       behind it, hiding several subtle problems and usually two decoys,
       with a decision to make.
   * - Audits and reviews
     - 12
     - An account, setup or report to audit or review, with planted
       problems, decoys and one clean control.

105 of the cases are generated by scripts. Each script simulates the
exports with a fixed seed, computes the true answer in code, checks that
the naive answer differs from it, and writes the graders with the
computed numbers in them. The answer key and the data cannot drift
apart. The other five were written by hand. All the data is synthetic.

The graders are LLM judges, each carrying its case's ground truth:
whether the planted problems were found with evidence, whether the
decision was right, whether a decoy was flagged. A run's score is the
weighted share of its graders that passed.

What it has shown so far
------------------------

One run has been made, on 2026-10-17: the 36 breadth cases, three runs
per case per arm, with Claude Haiku 5.5 in Claude Code. The committed
results:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * -
     - With Devil's Analyst
     - Without
   * - Mean score (0 to 1)
     - 0.94
     - 0.93

The difference, +0.01, is within noise at three runs per arm. **The
benchmark does not yet show that the plugin improves the answers.** The
model without the plugin already found most of the planted problems in
these cases. A Devil's Analyst skill fired in 107 of the 108
with-plugin runs, so the skills did load. The 62 focused cases and the
12 audits and reviews have not been run yet. Per-case results are in
`benchmarks/results/2026-10-17-breadth.md
<https://github.com/C-Kapsalis/devils-analyst/blob/v0.1.0/benchmarks/results/2026-10-17-breadth.md>`_.

A smaller spot check, not yet committed to the repository, was run on
2026-10-22 on three cases outside the breadth group: ``ab-srm``,
``pricing-discount-break-even`` and ``audit-ga4-utm``. It used one
Claude Haiku run per arm, graded with the cases' own graders. With the
skills, 10 of 10 graders passed; without them, 5 of 10. That is too
small a sample to generalize from, and the ``audit-ga4-utm`` result may
be inflated: the ``audit`` skill lists that case's planted problems as
examples.

Where the numbers can mislead you
---------------------------------

- **Synthetic data.** Every export was simulated, with problems planted
  on purpose. Real exports are messier, and the hardest questions (is
  this the right goal? the right margin?) cannot be planted.
- **Clean exports flatter the model.** Without the plugin, the model
  already avoided most single-number traps when the export was clean
  and the question pointed at the trap.
- **Three runs per arm.** One run moves a case's pass rate by a third.
  Read differences of a few points as noise.
- **An LLM judge.** The graders carry the computed answer, but a judge
  can still pass a vague report or fail a correct one. Agreement with a
  human grader has not been measured.
- **One model, one host.** The numbers are for Claude Haiku in Claude
  Code. The other agents load the same method but are not
  benchmarked.
- **Same author.** One person wrote the cases and the skills. Cases from
  other people would make the numbers more trustworthy. See
  :doc:`../contributing`.

Run it yourself
---------------

The cases, the graders and the scripts are all in the repository, so
you can rerun the benchmark or add a case of your own. The commands are
in :doc:`../contributing`.
